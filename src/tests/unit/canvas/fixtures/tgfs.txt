## Results

### Genetic structure and common factor model

To characterize the shared genetic structure of thyroid-related traits, we analyzed GWAS summary statistics for thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) levels, goiter, thyroid cancer, and Graves’ disease using linkage disequilibrium score regression (LDSC) and genomic structural equation modeling (Genomic SEM). The SNP-heritability estimates were 0.1057 (SE = 0.0105) for TSH, 0.0063 (SE = 0.0023) for goiter, 0.0069 (SE = 0.0017) for thyroid cancer, and 0.0085 (SE = 0.0015) for Graves’ disease (Table S2). Pairwise LDSC estimates showed small-to-moderate positive genetic correlations among the three disease traits, whereas TSH was negatively correlated with goiter and thyroid cancer and showed a near-zero correlation with Graves’ disease (Figure 2 and Table S3). Among the three disease traits, the genetic correlations were 0.4883 (SE = 0.2280) between goiter and thyroid cancer, 0.2233 (SE = 0.1241) between goiter and Graves’ disease, and 0.1785 (SE = 0.0508) between thyroid cancer and Graves’ disease. TSH showed negative genetic correlations with goiter (r_g = −0.2775, SE = 0.1249) and thyroid cancer (r_g = −0.2389, SE = 0.0935), whereas its correlation with Graves’ disease was near zero (r_g = −0.0253, SE = 0.0529). The common-factor model yielded χ² = 3.5544 with 2 degrees of freedom (P = 0.1691), a CFI of 0.9607, and an SRMR of 0.0383 (Table S4). All four thyroid-related traits had positive standardized loadings on the common factor (Table S5). The standardized loadings were 0.2995 for TSH, 0.7951 for goiter, 0.7347 for thyroid cancer, and 0.2305 for Graves’ disease. Loading uncertainty was SE = 0.0975 with P = 0.0021 for TSH, SE = 0.2829 with P = 0.0049 for goiter, SE = 0.1622 with P = 5.92 × 10⁻⁶ for thyroid cancer, and SE = 0.0782 with P = 0.0032 for Graves’ disease.
